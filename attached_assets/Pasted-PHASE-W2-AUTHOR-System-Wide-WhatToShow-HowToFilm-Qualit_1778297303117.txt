PHASE W2-AUTHOR — System-Wide WhatToShow + HowToFilm Quality Standard

Objective:
Upgrade the system-wide authoring behavior so every generated idea has a high-quality `whatToShow` and `howToFilm`, not just manually repaired W2-N entries.

This is NOT a 53-entry content patch.
This is NOT just W2-N.
This is NOT just validator cleanup.

The goal:
On every generation, every user-facing idea should include:
- a clear, funny, human `whatToShow`
- a simple, useful, filmable `howToFilm`

The target style is the V2 editorial standard:
- human contradiction first
- clear visual scene
- object/action carries the joke
- simple creator filming direction
- no generic boilerplate
- no “Phone on a tripod”
- no “locked-off shot”
- no “film yourself reacting”
- no vague “let the joke land”
- no instruction-manual copy
- no AI-ish filler

Examples of target quality:

WHAT TO SHOW:
Creator arrives at the door carrying every grocery bag in one trip like this is a moral victory. Instead of putting anything down, they try to open the door with their chin, fail twice, then stand there trapped by their own pride.

HOW TO FILM:
Keep all the bags visible. Let the chin attempts get slightly worse each time, then end frozen at the door like the groceries are now your personality.

WHAT TO SHOW:
Creator walks toward the chair like they are finally about to sit down, then remembers the clean laundry has been living there for three business days. They place their laptop on top of the pile like they are turning the chair into an office.

HOW TO FILM:
Start with the almost-sit. Stop before contact, look at the laundry, then slowly add the laptop like this was the plan all along.

Product standard:
Each generated card must be individually excellent.
Passing validators is the floor, not the goal.

TASK 4 — Define the new quality contract

Propose a clear internal contract for all future generated ideas:

whatToShow must:
- describe the full visible skit
- include setup, action, turn, payoff
- match the hook
- name the main object/action
- be easy to understand in one read
- feel like a real human moment
- avoid abstract filler

howToFilm must:
- tell the creator how to shoot it
- start with concrete framing or what to keep visible
- describe the key action
- describe the ending/payoff
- be 1–2 sentences
- be shootable in 10–15 minutes
- not repeat whatToShow word-for-word
- not introduce a new premise
- not use generic boilerplate
